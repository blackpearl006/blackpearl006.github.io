THE PROBLEM — VISIBILITY

Getting your work seen is hard

  • arXiv got 40,363 submissions in September 2026.
  • Since October 1, arXiv takes at most 2 submissions per author per month.
  • In computer vision and ML, many NeurIPS and CVPR papers come with a project website.
  • Why not papers in Nature or Science Advances? Or from The Neuro and our hospitals?

💡 Your paper's figures are already great website material.

Sources: arXiv monthly submission statistics (arxiv.org/stats/monthly_submissions); F. Jones, "arXiv's new preprint submission cap divides opinion", Times Higher Education, 2 Oct 2026.

  • There are too many papers. arXiv now even limits how many you can submit.
  • CV people solved this years ago: every big paper has a nice project page.
  • Neuro papers have amazing figures. They sit in a PDF that nobody opens.

HOW IT WORKS

Make it yours in three steps

  1. Put your results in a CSV. One row per region: region,value. Often it's already in your supplement.

  2. Give your agent the paper and AGENTS.md. Or edit the text files by hand. No code.

  3. Push to GitHub. GitHub Pages puts it online.

✨ Key takeaway: Give it your paper → get a website.

Give it your paper. Get a website.

  • No React knowledge needed. All content lives in Markdown and CSV files.
  • There is an AGENTS.md runbook. Your agent reads your paper and fills in the site. Rule one: never invent numbers.
WHY THIS MATTERS

Why this is a good job for AI

  • No human data. Only processed results that are already published.
  • Low stakes. If a page breaks, nobody gets hurt.
  • No maintenance. It only has to stay online.
  • Without AI, I would not have built this. I'd have minded my own business.
  • This is the core of my optimistic point. Not every AI use is risky.
  • Nobody will review or maintain a paper website. It just needs to exist and be accessible.
  • For me, the choice was not "AI site vs. hand-made site". It was "AI site vs. no site".
